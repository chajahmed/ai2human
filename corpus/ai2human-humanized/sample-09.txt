Introduction
Electric vehicles are now spreading fast worldwide, and they bring fire risks that existing city safety codes never accounted for. Lithium-ion batteries, which power nearly every major EV platform, can enter thermal runaway, a self-sustaining exothermic reaction where temperatures get above 700 °C. The reaction skips between cells faster than standard protective gear can manage, giving off dangerous byproducts like hydrogen fluoride and carbon monoxide. Volatile organic compounds at levels that threaten anyone inside a building. An EV battery fire behaves nothing like a regular car fire. It reignites on its own, sometimes hours after crews think it is out, and it tends to happen where firefighters have the hardest time working: underground garages, enclosed multi-storey charging decks, and tight urban charging lots with poor airflow (Elcin Eroglu, 2026). But this confined-space problem does not stop at city parking structures; it applies to any enclosed area where battery-electric cars sit or charge (Elcin Eroglu, 2026).
