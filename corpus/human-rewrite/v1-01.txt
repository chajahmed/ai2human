Go to any large hospital these days, and you will not only find the typical flow of nurses, doctors, and rolling carts, you'll also find a new addition: a computer. There's also the subtle, invisible presence of AI. It sounds like something out of a science fiction film, but AI has already found its place in one of the most critical medical tools today. For long, doctors had to depend on their own knowledge, experience and sometimes on their gut instinct to know what is wrong with a patient. Now they have a super-intelligent assistant which can filter through mountains of medical data in a blink of an eye. There's a huge paradigm shift from being reactive when someone gets sick to being proactive in preventing someone from getting sick. And the best part? It's personalizing and making healthcare more accessible for all.
