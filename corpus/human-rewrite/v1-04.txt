Modern automation is the realm of specialized computing devices which are designed to control machines, processes and environments. Two major technologies that often compete or sometimes even complement one another are the Programmable Logic Controller (PLC) and the Direct Digital Controller (DDC). Although both are designed to automate control loops and to control input/output (I/O) signals, they are the result of very different evolutionary paths, optimized for different environments and with very different philosophies. The PLC was developed from the rough and tumble world of industry, where complex relay logic circuits were used on factory floors, and was intended to supplant their use. DDC, however, was a product of the building science and environmental engineering field with its emphasis on indoor climate comfort, efficiency and safety. To truly value the distinction between PLCs and DDCs, one needs to delve into their history, their basic architectures, their communication protocols, their programming languages and their typical applications in order to see how this is not just a mere side by side comparison of two different commodities, but two different tools for different parts of the industry.
