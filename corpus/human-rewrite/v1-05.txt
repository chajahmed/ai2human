The difference really needs to be understood in the context of history of these technologies. The PLC was developed in the late 1960's, directly in response to the needs of the American automotive industry. General Motors was looking for a "standard machine controller" that would be able to be reprogrammed without an extensive hardware changeout when the new car model arrived, as it was too difficult and expensive to rewire complex relay-based control panels every time. This innovation by Dick Morley and his Bedford Associates colleagues was the first PLC, Modicon 084. This piece was designed specifically for use on a manufacturing floor, where it will be exposed to extreme temperatures, electrical noise produced by the heavy equipment, vibration and dust. Its primary job was to control high-speed, repetitive logic (discrete control) for conveyors, robotic arms and machining equipment. Conversely, the DDC began in the 1980's as pneumatic and electronic analog control systems for Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning (HVAC) have evolved. The energy prices rose sharply and the management of buildings became more complex, so that it was increasingly necessary to have more sophisticated, centralized control. The older pneumatic actuator systems were phased out and replaced by digital systems (DDC) that could be provided with complex scheduling, setpoint optimization, and centralized alarm management from one building management workstation. Both had very different backgrounds: One from a high speed world of manufacturing and the other from a comfort world of building management, and this difference set the design priorities in opposing ways from the beginning.
