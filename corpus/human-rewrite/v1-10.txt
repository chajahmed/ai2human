What is complicated by this identity-based threat model is a proven trend of indirect compromise via trusted third parties. Major supply chain and third-party breaches have quadrupled in the last five years, and the use of public facing applications has risen 44 percent year-over-year, according to IBM's X-Force Threat Intelligence Index. When an attacker accesses one trusted vendor's environment using stolen authentication tokens to gain access to a completely different environment, borders of 'your' attack surface have been pushed far into the software supply chain. Significantly, IBM researchers believe that much of this exposure isn't due to new attack methods but because basic security controls aren't consistently and consistently deployed at scale – especially when combined with a proliferation of identities and tools being deployed without governance. This is an important distinction for any thesis about 2026 cybersecurity: the most advanced AI security tools only work when basic cybersecurity practices – like proper configuration management and accurate asset inventories – are not followed.
