A typical fire safety architecture for a building level digital twin integrates 3D building geometry, IoT sensor streams and fire simulation tools. One implementation demonstrated by Yen and Lin (2024) is an evacuation signage system in multi-floor, multi-exit buildings that dynamically changes the signage of the evacuation route based on real-time sensor information instead of pre-programmed evacuation plans (F). To predict the severity of urban fires at a city level, Lee et al. (2025) added to the prediction element by using ensemble machine learning to forecast the weather patterns and spatial data to forecast fire severity (F). By using generative AI and machine learning trained on vented gas emissions from batteries to predict the fire risk of lithium-ion batteries in underground car parks (L/F), Aery et al. (2025) have shown that a digital twin is only as predictive as the hazard it is coupled with — and that, specifically, a generic building digital twin lacks of EV-specific gas emission features.
