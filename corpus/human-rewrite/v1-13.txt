Empirical characterisation of thermal runaway dynamics gives a "ground truth" set of hazard parameters necessary for validation of any predictive model. Ma et al. (2026) experimentally characterised the thermal runaway fire in commercial EV battery packs (L) and quantified the heat release rate, toxic gas emission profile (including hydrogen fluoride, carbon monoxide, and volatile organic compounds) and flame spread behaviour in representative charging scenarios. This is a set of measurements used to validate the prediction layer of the proposed tool. Jeong (2024) complemented this experimental characterisation by an alternative, a simulation-based method in which a Deep Operator Network (DeepONet) was trained on multiphysics thermal runaway simulation libraries (S) that could be used to make rapid continuous predictions without simulation for each specific case within a parameterized operational scenario space. The two methods are complementary: while Ma et al. (2026) used full-scale experiments to characterize the structure, it is prohibitive to repeat this testing for all possible operational conditions that a deployable risk tool needs to account for; Jeong (2024)'s DeepONet method has the advantage of being free of experimental cost but does require empirical data to train the model. The multi-scale nature of the hazard was put to rest by Zhang et al. (2025) who gave a detailed cell-to-system review from fire risk of the lithium-ion cell chemistry to thermal management at the pack level up to the regulatory requirements for systems (R).
