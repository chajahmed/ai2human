Machine learning techniques have been directly used to improve the operational outcomes of EV charging infrastructure risk assessment. To address this, Su et al. (2023) used machine learning models trained with operational and environmental parameters of batteries to classify the fire risk level of battery-operated car parks containing EV charging infrastructure (F), which provided a basis for a viable real-time fire risk classification in the worst-case EV charging scenario. Wang et al. (2024) created a quantitative risk index for large scale EV charging stations that incorporates station capacity, ventilation conditions and suppression system adequacy (F/S), and offers a structured risk assessment framework for compare and contrast analyses of EV charging infrastructure deployments. One key design feature of the proposed tool is the ability to monitor the charging network using a machine learning early-warning tool developed by Cheng et al. (2025) that sends signals over the charging network, rather than relying on vehicle-internal battery management systems. This was extended to a federated learning framework for privacy-preserving collaborative fault warning across thousands of EVs at charging stations (F) by Yang et al. (2026) to provide city-scale thermal anomaly detection while meeting the data privacy requirements. What is both interesting and important to note is the difference between infrastructure side monitoring and federated vehicle side aggregation as suggested by Cheng et al. (2025) and Yang et al. (2026), respectively, because they make different assumptions about the availability of data and privacy constraints, and the tool needs to be designed to make a choice and/or demonstrate that both can be integrated.
