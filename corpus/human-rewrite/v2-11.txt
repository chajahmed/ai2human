Meanwhile the super computer isn't only for science and research, it is also a part of everyday life. For example, in the medical field, they are used to identifying new drugs by studying the interactions between various chemicals in the human body. This accelerates discovery of treatments for diseases and even can help predict the spread of diseases. Supercomputers are also used in other industries, like engineering and transportation, to test designs before they are built, without having to make a costly prototype such as a new airplane or safer car. Nevertheless, there are problems associated with the use of supercomputers. They need a tremendous amount of electricity to be used, and people fear of consuming electricity and the environment. They're also quite costly, and not everyone can afford them. Despite these hurdles, work is underway to ensure supercomputers become more widely accessible via cloud computing and more energy efficient. With the evolution of technology, the importance of supercomputers will continue to increase, enabling us to tackle global challenges and enhance the lives of people around the world.
