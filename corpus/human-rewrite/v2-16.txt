The printing press, which was invented in the 15th century by Johannes Gutenberg, is the earliest mass communication device. It paved the way for mass printing of books and newspapers, spreading knowledge and threatening the power of religious and political establishments. The 19th and 20th centuries brought additional revolutions as this was followed by the invention of telegraph, radio, and television. Radio provided live news and entertainment to millions of people, and television, with its visual and auditory components, became one of the most powerful media with a worldwide impact, fostering a culture of shared experiences. They were also the traditional channels of mass communication that helped to build national identity, rouse people during wartime and organize social movements like the civil rights movement.
