The global fleet of electric vehicles exceeded 40 million units in 2024 and continues to expand at compound annual growth rates that are straining urban charging infrastructure. Regulatory timelines — including the European Union’s 2035 mandate for zero-emission new vehicles and analogous legislation across North America, China, and Southeast Asia — ensure that EV density within urban environments will continue to rise substantially over the next decade. This transition introduces a category of fire risk that existing safety codes, designed around hydrocarbon fuels and conventional electrical hazards, were not built to address. Lithium-ion batteries, which power all major EV platforms, can undergo thermal runaway: a self-reinforcing exothermic reaction that exceeds 700 °C, spreads from cell to cell before standard alarms activate, and generates toxic gases including hydrofluoric acid, carbon monoxide, and volatile organic compounds (Ma et al., 2026).
