Thermal runaway is the primary safety failure mode for lithium-ion batteries in EV applications, and its detection constitutes the foundational input signal required by any dynamic fire risk system — positioning this theme within the battery-level hazard layer of the three-layer conceptual framework introduced in Section 1. The methods reviewed here generate the electrochemical risk signals that the proposed tool’s LSTM component must process in real time; their limitations therefore directly define the input constraints of the proposed tool. Methods have progressed from simple threshold monitoring to multi-signal fusion architectures achieving sub-second warning lead times (Ye et al., 2025), yet reliable real-time warning across diverse battery chemistries and operational platforms remains an open problem. The thirteen studies reviewed in this section fall into four technical sub-areas: data-driven state estimation and fault diagnosis; multi-signal fusion for early warning; recurrent and temporal deep learning for sequence prediction; and physics-informed, graph neural network, and interpretable approaches. A persistent tension runs across all four sub-areas: architectures optimised for controlled laboratory conditions consistently underperform when evaluated against real-world deployed datasets, and performance benchmarks are difficult to compare across independent studies due to the absence of standardised benchmark datasets (Zhang et al., 2023). This benchmarking gap is not merely a methodological inconvenience; it means that architectural recommendations cannot yet be made with confidence for deployment across different battery chemistries and vehicle platforms.
