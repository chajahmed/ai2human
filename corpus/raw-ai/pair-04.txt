Across data-driven fault diagnosis approaches, hybrid CNN-LSTM architectures have consistently outperformed single-type baselines on multi-class battery fault classification under data-scarce conditions, while transformer-based models show emerging advantages for long-range state-of-health estimation — yet cross-chemistry generalisation remains unachieved by any architecture, limiting the deployment relevance of controlled-condition benchmarks reviewed here. These methods provide the diagnostic foundation on which early warning architectures are built. Sun (2023) proposed a hybrid architecture combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, applying Pearson correlation to rank input signals before training. The resulting feature-selective architecture outperformed single-type architectures on multi-class fault classification, particularly under limited training data conditions — a practically significant finding given the scarcity of labelled fault data in deployed EV fleets. Zhang et al. (2022) approached the problem from the state-of-health dimension, demonstrating accurate capacity estimation and early detection of degradation trajectories that precede thermal events, suggesting that fault detection and health estimation are converging into a unified diagnostic pipeline rather than remaining separate tasks.
