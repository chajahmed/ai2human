LSTM networks and their variants remain the dominant architecture for thermal runaway prediction from time-series sensor data, though more recent hybrid approaches have demonstrated substantial performance advantages under controlled experimental conditions. Zhang et al. (2023) confirmed that recurrent models consistently identify characteristic thermal runaway onset signals in both simulated and experimental data, while noting that without standardised benchmark datasets, architectural comparisons across independent studies cannot be reliably interpreted — a qualification that applies to the performance figures reported throughout this sub-area.

  Wang et al. (2026) tested a TCN-Transformer hybrid on 314 Ah lithium iron phosphate batteries across five state-of-charge (SOC) levels, reducing RMSE by more than 50% relative to LSTM, Bi-LSTM, and RNN baselines — a margin verified by Diebold-Mariano testing at 95% confidence (laboratory experimental study; physically instrumented 314 Ah LFP module; no independent field validation reported; benchmark not publicly available for replication). The experimental data yielded operationally significant calibration figures: thermal runaway onset temperature declined from 117.0 °C at low SOC to 101.9 °C at high SOC, and the vent-to-peak interval compressed from 2,013 s to 816 s as SOC increased. These results establish that detection thresholds must track battery state-of-charge in real time rather than treating the battery as a static system, and that the temporal window available for warning intervention is substantially shorter at high charge states — a constraint with direct implications for the response time requirements of any integrated fire risk tool. Jia (2025) conducted full-vehicle thermal runaway propagation experiments on a 100 kWh BEV, tracing cell-to-module-to-pack propagation and quantifying the hazard parameters that embedded detection systems must respond to within the available warning window. Ma (2023) demonstrated a practically viable alternative: a deep learning model with an attention mechanism in the time-frequency domain applied to dual voltage-temperature channels, with box-plot-derived thresholds triggering alarms. This approach proved functional in production EV environments (field/observational study using production vehicle data; two-signal input only) where the dense sensor instrumentation of Jia’s (2025) experimental rig is unavailable — confirming that practical deployment constraints limit architecture choice independently of theoretical performance, and identifying a necessary trade-off between sensor completeness and deployment feasibility.

  The apparent contradiction between LSTM’s operational dominance (Machlev, 2024) and the substantial benchmark superiority of transformer-based architectures (Wang et al., 2026; Le et al., 2026) reflects deployment constraints rather than a fundamental performance reversal: LSTM’s computational efficiency, mature embedded toolchain support, and suitability for battery management system hardware make it the de facto operational architecture, while transformer advantage is confined to controlled benchmark conditions where computational resources are unconstrained and sufficient labelled training data is available.
