This identity-centric threat model is compounded by a documented rise in indirect compromise through trusted third parties. IBM's X-Force Threat Intelligence Index reports that major supply chain and third-party breaches have quadrupled over the past five years, alongside a 44 percent year-over-year increase in the exploitation of public-facing applications. Incidents in which attackers used compromised authentication tokens from one trusted vendor to reach an entirely separate organization's environment illustrate how the boundaries of "your" attack surface now extend deep into the software supply chain. Notably, IBM researchers attribute much of this exposure not to novel attack techniques but to a persistent failure to implement basic security controls consistently at scale, worsened by identity sprawl and tools deployed without ongoing governance. This is a crucial nuance for any thesis on 2026 cybersecurity: the most sophisticated AI-driven defenses offer limited protection if foundational hygiene, such as consistent configuration management and accurate asset inventories, is neglected.
