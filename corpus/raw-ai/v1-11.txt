The standard architecture for a building-level digital twin in fire safety combines three-dimensional building geometry, IoT sensor streams, and fire simulation tools. Yen and Lin (2024) demonstrated one implementation: an evacuation signage system for multi-floor, multi-exit buildings that dynamically updates route guidance from live sensor data rather than static predetermined plans (F). Lee et al. (2025) extended the prediction element to city scale, applying ensemble machine learning to weather patterns and spatial data to forecast urban fire severity and support emergency resource allocation (F). Aery et al. (2025) connected both scales to the EV-specific hazard by applying generative AI and machine learning trained on vented gas emissions to predict lithium-ion battery fire risk in underground car parks (L/F), demonstrating that a digital twin is only as predictive as the hazard model with which it is coupled — and that EV-specific gas emission features are precisely what generic building digital twins currently lack.
