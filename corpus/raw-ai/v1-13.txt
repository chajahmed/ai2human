Empirical characterisation of thermal runaway dynamics provides the ground-truth hazard parameters against which any predictive model must be validated. Ma et al. (2026) experimentally characterised thermal runaway fire in commercial EV battery packs (L), quantifying heat release rates, toxic gas emission profiles — including hydrogen fluoride, carbon monoxide, and volatile organic compounds — and flame spread dynamics across representative charging scenarios. These measurements provide the validation reference data for the proposed tool’s prediction layer. Jeong (2024) complemented this experimental characterisation with a simulation-based alternative, training a Deep Operator Network (DeepONet) on multiphysics thermal runaway simulation libraries (S), enabling rapid continuous prediction across a parameterised operational scenario space without case-by-case simulation. The two approaches are complementary: Ma et al.’s (2026) full-scale experimental characterisation is invaluable for ground-truth validation but too costly to replicate across the full range of operational scenarios a deployable risk tool must cover; Jeong’s (2024) DeepONet addresses this cost constraint but requires empirical data for initial training. Zhang et al. (2025) provided a comprehensive cell-to-system review spanning fire risk from lithium-ion cell chemistry through pack-level thermal management to system-level regulatory compliance requirements (R), establishing the multi-scale nature of the hazard that any integrated tool must address.
