Machine learning methods have been applied directly to EV charging infrastructure risk assessment with demonstrable operational results. Su et al. (2023) deployed machine learning models trained on battery operational and environmental parameters to predict fire risk levels in underground car parks housing EV charging infrastructure (F), demonstrating viable real-time risk classification in the highest-risk EV charging environments. Wang et al. (2024) developed a quantitative risk index for large-scale EV charging stations accounting for station capacity, ventilation conditions, and suppression system adequacy (F/S), providing a structured framework for comparative risk assessment across charging infrastructure deployments. Cheng et al. (2025) developed a machine learning early warning method for charging thermal runaway that continuously analyses signals transmitted across the charging network (F), providing warnings from charging infrastructure data independently of vehicle-internal battery management systems — an important design choice for the proposed tool, since infrastructure-side monitoring does not require vehicle-embedded sensors. Yang et al. (2026) extended this to a federated learning framework for privacy-preserving collaborative fault warning across thousands of EVs at charging stations (F), achieving city-scale thermal anomaly detection while satisfying data privacy constraints. The distinction between Cheng et al.’s (2025) infrastructure-side monitoring and Yang et al.’s (2026) federated vehicle-side aggregation represents a genuine methodological divergence: the two approaches make different assumptions about data availability and privacy constraints, and the proposed tool must make an explicit choice between them or demonstrate that both can be incorporated.
