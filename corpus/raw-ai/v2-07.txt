The evidence gathered here supports the thesis that cybersecurity in 2026 is defined by the convergence of artificial intelligence, identity, cryptographic transition, and regulatory accountability into a single, resilience-oriented discipline. Artificial intelligence has become the decisive variable on both sides of the conflict, identity has replaced the network perimeter as the primary battleground, post-quantum cryptography has moved from academic concern to boardroom priority, and regulation has transformed cybersecurity from a technical function into an enterprise governance obligation. The organizations best positioned to navigate this landscape will not be those with the single most advanced tool, but those that combine AI-driven detection with disciplined identity governance, cryptographic agility, and demonstrable, auditable resilience. Future research should focus particularly on how governance frameworks can keep pace with autonomous AI agents acting as identities within enterprise systems, since this is likely to be the area of greatest structural risk beyond 2026.
