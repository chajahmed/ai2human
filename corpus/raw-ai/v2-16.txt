The history of mass communication can be traced back to the invention of the printing press by Johannes Gutenberg in the 15th century. This breakthrough made it possible to produce books and newspapers on a large scale, democratizing knowledge and challenging the authority of religious and political institutions. The 19th and 20th centuries witnessed further revolutions with the emergence of telegraph, radio, and television. Radio brought real-time news and entertainment into millions of homes, while television became a dominant medium that combined visual and auditory elements, creating shared cultural experiences across nations. These traditional forms of mass media helped create national identities, mobilize populations during wars, and promote social movements such as civil rights.
