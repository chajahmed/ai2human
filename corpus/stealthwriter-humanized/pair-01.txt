2.1 Data-Driven State Estimation and Fault Diagnosis
 Early data-driven approaches to battery safety framed the problem as fault detection within battery management systems. Sun (2023) proposed a hybrid architecture combining convolutional neural networks with long short-term memory, with Pearson correlation used to rank input signals prior to training. This feature-selected heterogeneous design outperformed single-architecture baselines on multi-class fault classification, particularly under training data scarcity — a finding that has direct relevance to the proposed tool, where labelled thermal runaway events will be sparse. Zhang et al. (2022) addressed state-of-health estimation rather than fault detection, demonstrating that capacity estimation accuracy sufficient to identify early degradation trajectories can serve as a precursor to thermal risk prediction. The convergence of fault detection and health estimation into a shared diagnostic pipeline, documented across both studies, reduces the sensor overhead required for the proposed tool’s prediction layer.
 S and Eswari (2025) proposed a hybrid framework combining CNNs and gradient boosting for continuous EV battery monitoring and anomaly detection (F), confirming that predictive analytics can be embedded directly within battery management loops without separate inference hardware. Machlev (2024) surveyed deep learning methods for EV battery fault diagnostics and prognostics (R), identifying ConvLSTM and feed-forward networks as the most widely deployed architectures, while noting three recurring obstacles: labelled fault data scarcity, explainability requirements for safety-critical deployment, and on-board computational constraints — all of which will constrain the LSTM component of the proposed tool. Shuhui et al. (2025) extended the diagnostic literature with a CNN that distinguishes the root cause of thermal runaway events — abuse mechanisms, ageing pathways, and internal short circuits — rather than simply detecting onset (L/S). Le et al. (2026) confirmed in an architectural review (R) that transformer-based models consistently outperform LSTMs for the long-range temporal dependencies underlying state-of-health estimation, though cross-chemistry and cross-manufacturer generalisation has not been demonstrated. This finding introduces a direct tension with the LSTM-centred architecture proposed here: the evidence from Le et al. (2026) suggests that a pure LSTM design may sacrifice long-range dependency capture for computational tractability, a trade-off that requires explicit acknowledgement and empirical validation in the proposed research.
