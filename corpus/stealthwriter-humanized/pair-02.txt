In 2026, the total number of EVs on the road worldwide exceeded 40 million, and their growth rate is compounding, putting an increasing strain on urban charging infrastructure. As a result of regulatory timelines, such as the European Union's commitment to adopting all zero-emission new vehicles by 2035 and similar laws in North America, China and Southeast Asia, we can expect the number of EVs in cities to increase significantly over the next ten years. This transformation brings with it a whole new class of fire hazard, to which current fire safety codes, which focus on hydrocarbon fuels and typical electrical risks, are not designed to be applicable. All major EV platforms run on lithium-ion batteries, which can have the ability to undergo thermal runaway a self-reinforcing exothermic reaction that exceeds 700 °C, spreads from cell to cell before standard alarms activate and creates toxic gases such as hydrofluoric acid, carbon monoxide and volatile organic compounds (Ma et al., 2026).
