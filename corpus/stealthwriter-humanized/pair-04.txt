Hybrid CNN-LSTM architectures have been consistently superior to single-type baselines in battery fault diagnosis on multi-class battery fault classification under data-scarce conditions, with transformer-based models being shown to have emerging benefits for the long-range battery state-of-health estimation problem, but neither transformer-based nor hybrid CNN-LSTM architectures have achieved cross-chemistry generalisation — making the challenges of controlled-condition benchmarks reviewed here less relevant for deployment. These methods can be used as the diagnostic building blocks for early warning architectures. Sun (2023) suggested a hybrid architecture of CNN and LSTM network with the input signals ranked by Pearson correlation before training. The resulting feature selective architecture proved superior to single type architectures in multi-class fault classification tasks, especially when faced with limited training data, a result of great practical significance as there is limited labeled fault data in deployed EV fleets. Turning to the state-of-health aspect of the problem, Zhang et al. (2022) showed how capacity estimation and fault detection/health estimation can be done accurately and how the latter can predict degradation trends before thermal failures, pointing to the fact that fault detection and health estimation are becoming a combined process instead of separate ones.
