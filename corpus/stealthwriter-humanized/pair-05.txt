Although more recent hybrid methods have shown significant performance gains with experimental conditions, LSTM networks and their variants are still the most popular architecture for thermal runaway prediction based on time-series sensor data. Zhang et al. (2023) found that the characteristic signals for the onset of thermal runaway are consistently detected in both simulated and experimental datasets, and they pointed out that the performance numbers presented in this sub-area cannot be readily interpreted when comparing to other independent studies, due to the lack of standardised benchmark datasets.

  The authors of Wang et al. (2026) performed a laboratory experimental study on a hybrid TCN-Transformer network on 314 Ah lithium iron phosphate batteries at five different states of charge (SOC) and achieved more than 50% improvement in RMSE compared to LSTM, Bi-LSTM, and RNN baseline networks, as verified by Diebold-Mariano testing at 95% confidence level, with no independent field validation reported and the network's benchmark not publicly available for replication. These experimental results gave operationally significant calibration values: the SOC had a direct effect on the thermal runaway onset temperature, which decreased from 117.0 °C at low SOC to 101.9 °C at high SOC; and the vent-to-peak interval decreased from 2,013 s to 816 s as SOC increased. These results also confirm that the detection thresholds need to be updated as the battery charge state changes, and not assume that the battery is a fixed system, and that the time window for warning intervention is significantly shorter at high charge states, which directly affects the response time requirements of any integrated fire risk tool. Jia (2025) performed full-vehicle thermal runaway propagation tests on a 100 kWh BEV, tracking the propagation of thermal runaway from the cell to the module to the pack, and measuring the hazard parameters that embedded detection systems should be able to detect within the warning time. Ma (2023) showed an practically viable alternative: a deep learning model with an attention mechanism in the time-frequency domain, coupled with thresholds computed from box-plot, which will trigger alarms. This method was shown to work in production EV environments (field/observational study using production vehicle data; two-signal input only) where the dense sensor instrumentation of the experimental rig used by Jia (2025) is not available, thus demonstrating that practical deployment constraints affect architecture choice, in addition to theoretical performance, and that a trade-off exists between sensor completeness and deployment feasibility.

  While LSTM is the operational architecture, the transformer-based architectures have significant benchmark advantages, this is not a fundamental performance shift but rather a consequence of deployment constraints: computational efficiency, mature embedded toolchain support and suitability for battery management system hardware make LSTM the de facto operational architecture, whereas the transformer advantage is limited to controlled benchmark conditions where computational resources are unlimited and there is enough labelled training data.
